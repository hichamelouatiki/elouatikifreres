MEP BIM & fluid coordination

MEP BIM: coordinating HVAC, plumbing, and electrical without silent clashes

June 14, 2023·12 min read

BIM deployment across technical packages (MEP) has transformed design coordination. Yet clearing hard geometric clashes in Navisworks or Solibri does not guarantee buildability or maintainability.

A federated model can show « zero collisions » while remaining unusable on site or in operations. This guide explains how to eradicate silent clashes through naming rules, LOD / LOIN management and weekly coordination reviews.

1. Anatomy of the MEP « silent clash »

Unlike a physical clash (a duct through a beam), a silent clash is a functional, regulatory or logical non-compliance: the software finds no intersection because volumes do not touch — yet the error is real.

Concrete example:a switchboard modelled accurately with a chilled water pipe 20 cm in front. Zero clash detected — yet doors cannot fully open and NF C 15-100 clearance rules are breached.
  1. Missing insulation: bare pipes without insulation thickness; once installed, networks overlap or block access.
  2. Gravity slopes ignored:wastewater modelled perfectly horizontal; applying the required 1–2 % slope triggers clashes or lowered ceilings.
  3. Inaccessible devices: valves, fire dampers or VAV boxes hidden behind cable trays, unmaintainable without major dismantling.

Golden rule: network flexibility hierarchy

  1. Gravity systems (wastewater, storm) — absolute priority (fixed slope).
  2. Large HVAC ducts — major bulk, costly to reroute.
  3. Pressurised piping (heating, cold/hot water) — offsets possible.
  4. Power / low current — most flexible if space is planned early.

2. The naming convention: first line of defence

Without ISO 19650-inspired nomenclature, the federated model becomes opaque. Every model and system needs a strict structure.

Model files: [PROJECT]-[ZONE]-[LEVEL]-[PACKAGE]-[DISCIPLINE]-[TYPE].ext
Example: PRV-BATA-R+1-CVC-GAI-EXE.rvt — HVAC ducts, Building A, Level 1, execution phase.

Systems in Revit / Nova:standardise abbreviations — CVC_SOU / CVC_REP, PLM_EF / PLM_EU / PLM_EP, ELE_CFO_BT / ELE_CFA_SSI. Never leave default software names (« Supply Air 2 »).

3. LOD / LOIN and maintenance zones

Over-modelling (bolts, manufacturer logos) saturates memory and hides real bulk issues. Under-modelling makes coordination unreliable. Contractual LOIN (Level of Information Need) is required in execution phase.

LOD matrix for MEP coordination

  • HVAC ducts — LOD 350:exact outside dimensions + virtual insulation volume (+50 mm).
  • Piping — LOD 350: real outside diameter, not nominal DN; space for tools and insulation.
  • Cable trays — LOD 300/350:30 cm free zone for pulling cables.
  • Switchboards — LOD 350:exclusion volume in front of doors (≈1 m clear depth).
  • Maintenance clearance boxes: transparent sub-volumes (MEP-Maintenance-Zone) in each equipment family; clash tests against other trades' rigid networks in Navisworks.

From the IFC model to structured data

Fluid coordination relies on structured data, not just pretty 3D renders. Every element (ID, category, material, dimensions) must be validated before federation — a prerequisite for targeted clash filters and reliable COBie exports.

Weekly coordination protocol

MEP coordination runs weekly, not in a monthly catch-up meeting. The BIM Coordinator compiles, filters and isolates silent clashes before the meeting — never a raw 4,500-clash dump.

  • Three-step method (2 h max):previous items status (20 min), dense-node resolution — plant rooms, ceiling plenums (80 min), closure and BCF assignment for next Friday (20 min).
  • BCF format: 3D view, object GUIDs, responsible package, clear description — via BIM Track, Revizto or equivalent on the CDE.

4. Typical weekly coordination agenda

Three milestones lock each package's accountability (HVAC, plumbing, electrical):

  • Friday 4pm

    Freeze and model upload

    • CDE upload: purged model (no construction lines), compliant naming.
    • Each package delivers its weekly EXE version before the weekend.

  • Monday morning

    BIM Coordinator analysis

    • Federation + targeted test matrices (e.g. cable trays vs ducts > 200 mm).
    • Pre-coordination report: hard clashes + silent clashes (maintenance zones, slopes, insulation).

  • Wednesday 10am

    Weekly review (2 h)

    • BIM Manager, Coordinator and package leads — design-team arbitration per network priority matrix.
    • No live design for 50 topics: focus on nodes not resolved asynchronously.

5. Case studies: critical coordination nodes

Two recurring situations where « zero clash » misleads the project team:

  • Hospital corridor plenum (2.20 m): smoke exhaust 600×400, supply air, power, low current, medical gases. Solution: altitude strata — heavy ducts at slab soffit, piping on trapeze supports, cables just above removable ceiling.
  • Central plant room: pumps and tanks with no geometric clash, yet headers above motors — impossible pump motor replacement in operations. Fix: offset headers + virtual lifting zone blocking small services.
  • Office corridors:group services on multi-trade supports instead of stacking « flat » in width only.
  • Operations: every coordination decision must anticipate preventive maintenance (HVAC filters, valve access, fire safety).

6. Conclusion: collective discipline before algorithms

MEP BIM success is not about clash-engine sophistication but project governance. Eradicating silent clashes requires a locked naming convention, controlled LOIN and weekly reviews focused on buildability and real operations.
